PRODUCT DESIGN INTERNSHIP · OXY · 2026

Oxy Design Agency

Designing across strategy, research, and visual design for a creative agency and its clients.

Role Product Design Intern
Duration Feb–Present

Overview

Over 4 months at Oxy, a Phoenix-based design agency, I've worked as a product design intern across client engagements spanning UX research, competitive analysis, information architecture, and visual design.

The two projects below share a thread: an investor, writer, and community builder with five careers and no website at all, and a luxury aviation studio reading like a SaaS product. Different problems, same job: closing the gap between the work and the story told about it.

PROJECT 01

Sadasia McCutchen - brand identity and website for a multi-hyphenate operator

The Brief

Five careers. One practice. No website.

Sadasia McCutchen is an investor at SignalFire who previously worked at Google. She writes, builds communities, and works in fragrance and fashion. The through-line she named was relationship infrastructure: not five careers, one practice of putting aligned people in the same room and keeping them there. The site had to show the range as a single practice, not a list of accomplishments.

Moodboard

Round one missed. The miss is what taught me.

I wasn't on the discovery call, so the first three moodboards came from sticky notes and fragments. None of them felt like her, and she said so. Warm Authority came closest, but she wanted more color, more plant life, and more play. Hearing her talk through the gap gave me what the notes never could.

Round two pushed exactly there. Bright Authority turned the color up: jewel tones, saturated portraiture, botanicals rendered like stained glass. She called it the perfect combination of playful, expert, and relatable, which was the exact tension the brief had been circling.

Identity System

The name was already the logo.

Sadasia's name was made up, given with intention, and built to go far. I tested five treatments in high-contrast editorial serifs and let the letterforms carry the identity.

Romie carries the whole site as she wanted one voice throughout, so range comes from weight and italics rather than a second typeface. The palette of linen, wine, chartreuse, terracotta, and sky holds jewel tones without going heavy. Two marks grouped together round it out: a key, because opening doors for people is what she actually does, and a flower, from her love of fragrance and plants.

Wireframes

Eight pages, three rounds. One did the hard thinking.

I wireframed all eight pages across three rounds, each adding real imagery so stakeholders could picture the site. On an image-dependent site, if a client can't picture the page, the wireframe hasn't done its job.

The Library was the structural problem: It was the only commerce surface, with products not yet final, so the structure had to flex. The Foyer and Parlor carried positioning weight; the Library had to carry uncertainty.

Choose a Room

Navigation had to prove the concept.

The rooms have to work as navigation before they work as anything else, so I designed three versions. A short dropdown gave visitors no reason to believe the rooms were real; a modal floor plan asked people to read a layout before they could go anywhere. The long dropdown shows each room with an image and description so you see the room before you enter it. Sadasia chose it, and it's the version I'd have argued for. I art directed AI interior imagery for all four rooms in the brand's jewel tones so they read as part of the site.

Style Tiles

Three directions, one flaw caught in round one.

I presented three hero directions in light and dark, but used the same portrait across all three. My supervisor caught that identical imagery made them read as variations, not choices. Round two gave each its own treatment.

1A hid her portrait below the fold on a site where she's the reason anyone visits; 1B put her name above her when the point is what she does with it. 1C frames the portrait inside an arch to resemble a doorway, so the room concept shows up in the structure of the page. Sadasia chose 1C.

Hi-Fi

The arch became a rule.

In the hero, the arch framed one portrait. Across the homepage it crops the dinner photograph, the closing call-to-action portrait, and all four rooms in the dropdown, so the concept holds without being explained.

Then the arch stopped being a crop and became a surface: Fabric texture sits behind her in the hero and the closing call to action, and lines the Choose a Room dropdown: upholstery, drapery, backdrops, and a nod to the fashion side of her work. Texture needed a limit, so I set one: brand surfaces and editorial imagery only. Her portraits and the products stay untreated, because a filter on a face reads as a filter on the credibility behind it.

What I Learned

Notes from a discovery call give you what the client said. They can't give you what she lit up about.

The first moodboard round missed because I designed from a transcript; the second landed because I designed from a conversation.

PROJECT 02

Oxy Aero - from research to visual direction for a luxury aviation design studio

The Brief

Oxy Aero had the work. The site didn't know it.

Oxy Aero is Oxy's own venture, a private aviation studio designing the entire client experience, from the first text to stepping off the plane. The website read like a SaaS company. In kickoff, Anthony named the bar as Amangiri: so confident it doesn't need to explain itself. My job was to close that gap.

Research

The work existed. The world didn't.

I analyzed three bespoke studios (Winch Design, Singer Vehicle Design, Ago Interiors) and audited the full site, logging 28 prioritized observations: functional errors, brand misalignments like a commercial aircraft wing in the hero, and missed opportunities like the one real project sitting in a gallery with no brief or outcome. The conclusion: the content needed to build trust already existed. This wasn't a rebuild. It was a translation problem.

Hero Design & Outcome

Three directions, one shared system.

I designed three hero directions across two rounds, all built on the same updated navigation and copy, so the choice came down to imagery and typographic register: a grounded jet at golden hour with centered editorial type, an aerial over open water with oversized italic display type, and a jet in the clouds with quiet left-aligned type and hairline grid marks.

All three pulled from a 14-image AI library I art directed against AI's defaults (overlit, eager to impress) because with one real client, the imagery had to assume you already belonged there.

Andrew and Spencer, Oxy's creative director, both landed on 1B, which Spencer called the closest to the Oxy parent brand: the same design system, but in a new register of restraint. That was the brief all along. Aero had to read as family, not as a departure.

What I Learned

Amangiri wasn't an aesthetic. It was a standard. Luxury at that level doesn't explain itself.

You only learn that by looking at enough of the right references until the pattern becomes impossible to miss.
